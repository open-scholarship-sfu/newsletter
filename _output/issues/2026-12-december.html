End-of-year reflections and looking ahead.

Hello, community! 🎄

How it’s December already? This time last year we were sharing some of our very first activities and the first blog post we’ve written with the Knowledge Mobilization Hub. One year later, we have participated in a handful of institutional, national or international events, launched our Open Scholarship Champions program, revamped our website, created a lot of resources, and, most importantly, we are more and more mindful and aware about our community needs and desires, our institutional infrastructure to support open scholarship practices, and learning together how to advocate for the culture change we want to see. And we are so, so happy that you’re sticking around with us!

This newsletter is now written, reviewed and published openly on GitHub, the same way we encourage open research. Every issue’s source, its review and what was sent are public, and anyone can reuse the template under a CC BY 4.0 licence.

Have news to share? An event, a project, a funding call or a resource: tell us through a short online form, and we’ll consider it for an upcoming issue. Celebrating Open Education at SFU

On November 10th we gathered at the Burnaby Library for a lunch to celebrate those using, producing, sharing or just curious about open educational resources and practices. We heard from Evan Acetolla about engaging students in producing open educational resources, from Juan Pablo Alperin about how his experience in the back-end of open publishing shaped his career and teaching practices, from Stephanie Bertels about the open educational products that have been used to improve companies’ sustainability strategies, and from Jessica Lamb about the impacts of textbooks costs on students’ education and how open resources can mitigate that. The event was co-organized with the Open Education Community of Practice.

We congratulate and appreciate all the open education champions in our community! Thank you! ♥️

We’ve been busy!

2nd Canadian Open Science Conference

In October the Open Scholar Champions from SFU joined a wider Canadian community on the 2nd Canadian Open Science Conference in Ottawa, ON. Our community shared insights about the intersections between AI and Open Scholarship and our strategy to map SFU’s resources and infrastructure to support open scholarship practices. Together with the TOSI Alliance, we presented results of two of our working groups: one about standards in neuro data sharing and another about curation of training resources for neuro open science.

SORTEE Conference 2026

We were also present at the 2026 edition of the global and online conference of the Society for Open, Reliable, and Transparent Ecology and Evolutionary Biology (SORTEE). Our community manager hosted an unconference where she provoked participants to analyse if we could use metrics and concepts from Ecology and Evolution to understand and evaluate open scholarship practices. Materials are openly published and can be reused under CC-BY license.

Resources

  • OS in the research life cycle: we have included on our website an extensive list of resources, infrastructure information and support guides to help you navigate open scholarship practices at SFU.
  • The Sharing Shift: the Open Knowledge Foundation and CNRS have launched a project to keep the digital commons open without leaving them open to extraction by AI. See you in 2027! ❄️
